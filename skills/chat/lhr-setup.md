## How to interview

Ask one question at a time and never bundle topics. After each answer, reflect it back in one or two sentences
("Got it, so the niche is X, aimed at Y") so they can correct you before you move on. When the answer space is
small, offer 3 or 4 lettered options and let them reply with a letter or their own words. Use an open question for
anything that is really prose. If they say they don't know, offer a sensible default and say what you assumed.

## The seven topics

1. **Identity and niche.** What does the business sell or publish, for whom, and what makes it different from a
   generic lifestyle store? Capture a 2 to 4 sentence niche statement.
2. **Audience and brand voice.** Who is the reader or customer, described as a persona (age, interests, shopping
   habits), and what tone should the writing have (3 to 5 adjectives or example phrases)?
3. **Content strategy.** Beyond recipes, which pillars (kitchen and tool reviews, entertaining and hosting guides,
   behind-the-scenes and brand story, seasonal roundups, or their own)? How tightly must content funnel toward a
   product, or is some content fine just for engagement? The working assumption is about 26 posts every 6 months;
   keep it or change it?
4. **Product and fulfillment.** Core product categories in priority order; the fulfillment model (owned
   inventory, dropship, print-on-demand, or hybrid); pricing philosophy (budget, premium and curated, or between);
   and whether there is a sets or bundles strategy tied to seasons or themes.
5. **Community and engagement roadmap.** Which of forums, giveaways and raffles, a newsletter, and comments on
   posts are near-term, and which are aspirational? As a solo owner, how much moderation time per week is
   realistic?
6. **Non-negotiables versus flexible conventions.** Looking back over topics 1 to 5, is anything a hard,
   rarely-changing principle (it would go in the project's constitution) rather than a convention that may
   evolve (it would go in the rules)? "Nothing, treat it all as flexible" is a valid answer.
7. **Chief-of-staff persona**, as five separate questions: formality of tone (direct and casual, professional but
   warm, or blunt); default verbosity (terse, or explanatory); humor tolerance (none, occasional dry humor, or
   playful); how much detail when it surfaces tradeoffs and risk (the headline only, or the full reasoning; it
   always surfaces them, this tunes how much); and preferred form of address plus any pet peeves.
